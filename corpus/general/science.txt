Science is a systematic way of studying the natural world. Scientists observe phenomena, create hypotheses, perform experiments, and analyze evidence.

The Earth is one of the planets in the Solar System. It travels around the Sun while also rotating around its own axis.

The Moon is Earth's natural satellite. The Moon travels around Earth and reflects light from the Sun.

Water is essential for life. It can exist as a solid, liquid, or gas depending on temperature and pressure.

Plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. Photosynthesis allows plants to produce chemical energy and release oxygen.

Gravity is a force that attracts objects toward one another. Earth's gravity keeps people and objects near its surface.

Light travels extremely quickly through empty space. Sunlight takes several minutes to travel from the Sun to Earth.

The human body contains many systems that work together. The nervous system helps coordinate information throughout the body.

Energy can take many forms, including thermal energy, electrical energy, chemical energy, and kinetic energy.

Scientific knowledge develops through observation, experimentation, measurement, and critical evaluation of evidence.
