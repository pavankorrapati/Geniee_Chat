Maya found an old notebook on a wooden table. The notebook contained several pages of handwritten observations about the stars.

Every evening, Maya looked at the sky from the small balcony outside her room. She wanted to understand why the stars appeared to move across the sky.

One night she noticed a bright object that seemed to move differently from the others. She wrote down the time, direction, and position in her notebook.

The next morning she searched through a science book and discovered that planets could appear to move differently from distant stars.

Maya realized that careful observation could reveal patterns that were difficult to notice at first.

She continued recording her observations every night. Over time, her notebook became a detailed record of the changing night sky.

The experience taught Maya an important lesson: curiosity becomes more powerful when it is combined with careful observation.

Arun wanted to learn programming, but his first program failed.

He read the error message several times and still could not understand it.

Instead of giving up, he divided the problem into smaller parts. First he checked the input. Then he checked the function. Finally he examined the line mentioned by the error.

He discovered that he had used the wrong variable name.

After fixing it, the program worked.

Arun smiled because the solution had been much simpler than he expected.

He learned that debugging is often a process of asking small questions until the source of a problem becomes clear.
